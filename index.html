Saving of Swedovia

A Simulation Game Exploring Ethical Dilemmas in Crisis Situations

Saving of Swedovia is a newly launched research project that uses a simulation game to explore how people navigate ethical dilemmas during crises. The project examines decision-making under pressure, when leaders must choose between competing values with no clear right answer.

Building on my previous work interviewing parliamentarians about political ethics, this project extends the research into crisis management—where the stakes are even higher and time for deliberation is limited.

Key Questions

  • How do ethical principles hold up under crisis conditions?
  • What role does interprofessional collaboration play in ethical decision-making?
  • Can simulations help train better crisis leadership?

Presented at the CICE Conference 2025 at Linnaeus University, bringing together researchers and practitioners in emergency care and crisis management.

When Political Ethics Meets Political Practice

Dilemmas, Virtues & Vices in the Swedish Parliament

For my PhD, I conducted in-depth interviews with Swedish parliamentarians, exploring how they navigate ethical dilemmas in their daily work. The central finding: politicians' biggest challenges stem not from choosing between good and evil, but from powerlessness—the constraints of party loyalty, limited resources, and institutional barriers.

Governance (2025)

A mixed-method study with 1,659 Swedish politicians exploring how transparency affects their cooperation with interest organizations. The findings reveal that politicians prioritize independent political judgment over transparency concerns.
